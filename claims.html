Claim vs Evidence

Each entry below takes a specific public claim and sets it directly against the exact wording of the source documents. The evidence status reflects what the documents actually say — no more, and no less — including where the record is still incomplete. More entries are added as they are researched and checked.

"The children's remains were dumped in a septic tank."

Where this claim has appeared
Widely repeated in national and international media coverage since 2017, and in general public discussion of the Tuam site.
Relevant source documents
Commission of Investigation statement, 3 March 2017 (Document Library, TRP-002); ODAIT forensic updates, 2025–2026 (TRP-010, TRP-011).
Exact source wording
"Test trenches were dug revealing two large structures. One structure appears to be a large sewage containment system or septic tank that had been decommissioned and filled with rubble and debris and then covered with top soil. The second structure is a long structure which is divided into 20 chambers. The Commission has not yet determined what the purpose of this structure was but it appears to be related to the treatment/containment of sewage and/or waste water... In this second structure, significant quantities of human remains have been discovered in at least 17 of the 20 underground chambers which were examined."

— Commission of Investigation, 3 March 2017

Plain-English explanation
The Commission's own 2017 statement describes two separate structures. The first — the one it explicitly calls a decommissioned "sewage containment system or septic tank" — was filled with rubble and covered over; no remains were reported found in it. The remains were found in the second structure, a 20-chambered underground feature whose original purpose the Commission said it had "not yet determined," beyond noting it appeared sewage- or wastewater-related. In short: the popular shorthand "septic tank" conflates the two structures. The precise nature of the chamber where remains were actually found — and how the remains came to be there — is something the Commission itself said was not yet established in 2017, and it remains a subject of the ongoing forensic excavation.
Evidence status
Partially supported, imprecisely stated — human remains were confirmed in an underground chamber structure, which is documented fact. Whether "septic tank" is an accurate description of that specific structure, and the manner and intent behind how remains came to be there, is not settled by the 2017 statement and is an area the ongoing 2025–2027 excavation is expected to clarify further.
Unresolved questions
What was the chamber structure's original function, and was it in active use during the period remains were interred? Who was responsible for placing remains there, and under what circumstances? These are explicitly still open, per ODAIT's own public updates.
